Case study · 2026

Nisha Perfume

A perfume store redesigned around how people actually choose a scent: website, iOS and Android app, and one design system for both.

Company
CodeGeeks
Role
Sole UX/UI Designer
Platforms
Website, iOS and Android app, design system
Status
Website released · live site↗ · app in development

The task

A store that didn’t help people choose

Nisha Perfume sells original and niche perfume in Ukraine, in full bottles and by the millilitre. The client came with a working website and app and wanted both improved, in UX and in UI.

The old store looked like a template and didn’t help people choose a scent:

  • banners in different styles, with no single message;
  • product cards with only a name and a price;
  • selling by the millilitre, the store’s strength, was hidden;
  • an Instagram block took the main place on the home page.

How I worked

From design sessions to handoff

I was the only designer on the project and worked in a full team with developers, QA and a project manager.

  1. Design sessions with the client to talk through problems and ideas.
  2. Competitor analysis on the Ukrainian market, to solve the client’s business problems and see how others work.
  3. Information architecture, agreed with the client before any design.
  4. UX audit of the old website and app.
  5. User flows, wireframes, UI and prototypes in Figma and Claude Design.
  6. Handoff to developers and checks that the build matches the design.

Key decisions

What changed and why

One clear message on the home page

ProblemThe home page had no focus: banners in different styles and an Instagram block in the main spot.

DecisionOne message in the hero, try any scent in a small volume; top brands and “New & bestsellers” right after it; dark warm banners with one green accent.

Choose a volume right in the catalog

ProblemSelling by the millilitre was hidden; cards showed only a name and a price.

DecisionVolume from 2 ml right in the catalog card, with price per ml, rating, reviews, favourites and a gender tag in one block. The card has three states: default, hover and volume selected.

Filters that match how people search

ProblemSeven closed dropdowns: people had to open each one to see what was inside.

DecisionAn A–Z brand index and favourite brands; chips for gender, volume, fragrance type, concentration and season; including and excluding notes (top, heart, base); a price per ml range.

A product page that explains the scent

ProblemPeople couldn’t tell how a perfume smells or how long it lasts before buying.

DecisionA note pyramid in plain words, longevity and sillage scales, “96% bought again”, reviews that show the purchased volume, and a sticky buy bar on mobile.

Checkout in three steps

ProblemA long checkout without a clear end.

DecisionCart, Checkout, Done, with the summary always in view; saved Home and Work addresses; Nisha Coins with one toggle and a slider; monobank, cards, Apple and Google Pay, instalments and cash on delivery.

Build your own set

ProblemPeople ordered perfume sets through Instagram: they had to write which scents they wanted, how many millilitres and how many vials.

DecisionA set builder on the website, a new feature the client asked for and we designed together. Pick a box (5 × 3 ml, 3 × 5 ml or 5 × 5 ml), add scents with search and filters, and the set summary with the total updates as you go.

Mobile app

A store that knows you

The iOS and Android app adds what a website can’t: a personal feed, gifts and loyalty in one place.

A short quiz, then a personal feed

Four quick steps: which scents, which families, favourite brands and when you plan to wear them. The app then picks scents for that taste and opens the “Specially for you” feed.

Hint about a gift

Right from the product page, people can send a partner or a friend a hint: who it’s for, their email, the occasion and a message, with a preview of the email.

Design system

One system for web, iOS and Android

I built it from scratch together with Claude: one set of tokens and components for the website and the iOS and Android app.

Foundations

Color, type and spacing tokens: a green accent on warm neutrals, Commissioner for headings and prices, Inter for the interface, and spacing on a 4 px grid.

Every component in every state

Components are drawn in all their states and sizes. The volume picker has default, hover, selected and disabled in four sizes; the note filter has a third state, exclude.

One system for the website and the app

The same mobile menu works in three versions: in the app, for a guest on the website and for a signed-in customer with their Nisha Coins.

Ready for developers

Shadow and z-index scales with clear rules, so developers use tokens instead of “magic” numbers.

Status

Website released

The new website went live in November 2026. The team is still building the app.
