Case study · 2025

Lumina Glow

An app that shows how food affects your skin: scan your face and your meals, and follow your skin week by week.

Company
CodeGeeks
Role
Sole UX/UI Designer
Platform
iOS and Android app
Scope
Onboarding, scans, skin dashboard, paywall

The task

A long onboarding worth finishing

Lumina Glow helps people understand how what they eat shows up on their skin. Before the app can help, it needs to know a lot about the person.

The goal was to make a long onboarding personal and worth going through, so people reach their plan:

  • many questions, without feeling like a form;
  • a face photo, which people are careful about;
  • value before the paywall, not after it;
  • a trial people can trust.

How I worked

From the client’s wireframes to the final UI

I was the only designer on the project. The client came with wireframes and early ideas; I designed the UI and improved the flows.

  1. Wireframes and ideas from the client as a starting point.
  2. Competitor analysis and a review of apps of this type.
  3. Regular discussions with the client about the flow and priorities.
  4. UI for every screen of the onboarding, scans, plan and paywall.
  5. Improvements to the flow: what to ask, in what order, and when to show value.

Key decisions

What makes it work

Six parts of one flow, from the first question to the trial.

One question per screen

A progress bar, one action and a short “why we ask” under sensitive questions. The name and goals come back later in the personal plan.

A face scan people feel safe with

Before the camera: why the photo is needed and how it is stored. Tips for a good photo: bright light, no filters, no makeup. The scan becomes the current skin score and a target, so progress has a goal.

Pain in pink, relief in green

Symptoms include feelings, not only skin: being judged, avoiding social life, stress. Every pain has a positive answer. Soft pink for the problem, the calm brand green for the solution.

A food scan you can read at a glance

A letter grade, three skin risks with scales, and every ingredient marked as good or bad. People can scan their first meal during onboarding or skip it.

A plan and a daily dashboard

A personal plan with a target date, reviews from people like you, and a daily dashboard with a weekly streak, face scan results by zone and what to eat.

An honest paywall

“No payment due now” above the button, a reminder before the trial ends, and a clear timeline: today, a reminder on day 2, billing on day 3. Monthly and yearly plans side by side.

What I’d test next

Hypotheses, not results

These are ideas for A/B tests, not measured results.

  1. PaywallShow the trial timelineA: only plans and price. B: today, a reminder on day 2, billing on day 3. Clear terms remove the fear of a hidden charge. Metric: trial start rate.
  2. OnboardingLet people try the food scan firstA: paywall right after the quiz. B: scan the first meal, then the paywall. A real result before paying makes the subscription feel worth it. Metrics: paywall conversion, drop-off at the scan.
